## The Speaker's Spoken Delivery

My name is Chandana, and I am from Hyderabad. To give you a brief overview of my educational background: I completed my schooling up to the 12th standard in Hyderabad, pursued my undergraduate degree in engineering at Birla Institute of Technology (BIT) Mesra, and subsequently completed my MBA from IIM Indore. Following business school, I worked in the private corporate sector at Deloitte for 14 months. I resigned from my job approximately one to two months prior to the Civil Services preliminary examination and appeared for my first attempt in UPSC CSE 2020. 

Through that 2020 attempt, my name appeared in the Reserve List (the second consolidated list issued following the main merit list), and I was allocated the Indian Corporate Law Service (ICLS). I did not sit for the examination in 2021. In 2022, I appeared for the examination a second time, securing All India Rank 331, which allocated me to the Indian Revenue Service (Income Tax) [IRS-IT]. While undergoing active foundational and professional service training, I sat for the examination a third time in UPSC CSE 2023, achieving All India Rank 50, which secured my allocation to the Indian Administrative Service (IAS). Across these three distinct attempts, I have navigated every stage of the examination cycle. Today, I want to share the practical, unfiltered truth regarding the strategies, resource choices, and psychological disciplines that governed my journey across Prelims, Mains, and the Personality Test.

Before touching advanced coaching materials or answer-writing test series, every serious aspirant must establish a rigorous foundation. Like most successful candidates, I began by reading the standard NCERT textbooks at least two to three times. 

However, reading alone is insufficient without a structured **Layered Revision Architecture**. Across all materials I studied—whether foundational textbooks or compilation booklets like Mains 365—I implemented a multi-pass color-coding system. During the initial pass, I read the text cleanly without making heavy markings. In subsequent passes, I annotated using distinctly colored pens: black, then blue, then pink or fluorescent highlighters. Each color denoted a specific revision cycle, ensuring that every page received five to six disciplined revisions. 

For the final consolidation pass, I affixed a single Post-It note to the top of the textbook page. On that Post-It, I recorded exclusively the high-friction facts, names, or provisions that my memory consistently failed to retain. Consequently, during the final days before the examination, I did not re-read hundreds of pages; I glanced purely at those compressed Post-It summaries.

My core standard bibliography was strictly minimal:
- **Polity:** M. Laxmikanth (revised exhaustively).
- **Modern Indian History:** Spectrum (*A Brief History of Modern India* by Rajiv Ahir).
- **Ancient & Medieval History:** Old NCERTs.
- **Environment:** Shankar IAS (selectively reading core conceptual chapters while deliberately skipping non-essential bulk).
- **Science & Technology:** The Sunday editions of *The Hindu*, specifically curating articles covering breakthroughs and applied sciences.

After five to six months of foundational reading, I initiated answer writing. During the initial phase, my answer practice was modest and sporadic. However, two to three months prior to the Preliminary examination, I pivoted into complete, dedicated Prelims immersion.

For both my 2020 and 2022 attempts, I completed **100 full-length mock tests**. Merely solving tests, however, is a vanity metric; the transformative leverage comes from maintaining a dedicated **Error Log Sheet**. Following every sectional and full-length mock, I recorded:
1. The total number of questions attempted.
2. The exact breakdown of correct answers versus incorrect answers.
3. An analytical error diagnosis explaining *why* an error occurred (e.g., careless misreading, factual ignorance, or flawed deductive guessing).
4. Subject-wise performance tracking.

By compiling this data across 100 mocks, I calculated my cumulative statistical averages: my optimal attempt range, my baseline accuracy rate, and the specific subjects where my intuitive educated guesses yielded positive net marks versus subjects where guessing caused negative penalties. Grounded in this empirical calibration, I entered the examination hall with absolute composure, scoring **118** in Prelims 2020 and **115+** in Prelims 2022, comfortably clearing the cutoff in both cycles.

Regarding **CSAT (General Studies Paper 2)**: Coming from an MBA background with prior CAT examination preparation, I had never required dedicated CSAT preparation in my initial attempts, comfortably securing 113 in 2020 and 112 in 2022. However, the 2023 CSAT paper delivered an unprecedented shock across the entire aspirant community. In that attempt, my score dropped to an estimated 70–75 range. 

This experience taught me a vital lesson: **never treat CSAT casually.** If I were preparing today, my non-negotiable strategy during the final seven weeks would be to solve at least one full-length CSAT mock test per week across seven different coaching institutes. Practicing tests from varied coaching brands exposes the candidate to distinct question framing and problem traps. The objective in CSAT is not perfection; it is guaranteeing a safe score of 80+ in mocks so that clearing the mandatory 66-mark qualifying threshold on examination day is insulated from unforeseen difficulty spikes.

Moving to the **Mains Examination**:
Many aspirants make the grave mistake of viewing Prelims and Mains as isolated silos. In reality, your Prelims factual repository must actively enrich your Mains analytical answers. 

Consider **General Studies Paper 1 (GS1)**. When addressing modern history questions, mere abstract narrative is mediocre. For example, when comparing Mahatma Gandhi and Rabindranath Tagore, you must incorporate precise historical facts to substantiate differences and commonalities: highlight Tagore’s internationalist philosophical orientation versus mass-mobilization anti-colonial struggle, and explicitly cite the Jallianwala Bagh massacre, demonstrating how both leaders surrendered their imperial honors (Tagore renouncing his knighthood and Gandhi returning his Kaiser-i-Hind medal). 

Furthermore, in GS1 Geography, visual schematics are compulsory. Whenever a question addresses an economic or industrial theme—such as the spatial distribution of the Indian sugar industry—you must immediately draw a clear map of India, accurately marking primary production clusters across Uttar Pradesh and Maharashtra. Geographical maps and structural flowcharts instantly elevate your presentation above generic paragraphs.

In **General Studies Paper 2 (GS2)**, where I secured a high score of 120, my primary strategic breakthrough was transitioning beyond standard constitutional provisions into **targeted judicial precedents and case laws**. Generic candidates cite Fundamental Rights, Directive Principles, and Fundamental Duties. High-scoring candidates cite precise Supreme Court jurisprudence:
- When answering questions on gender justice, explicitly invoke *Babita Puniya v. Union of India* regarding permanent commissions for women in the armed forces, alongside recent landmark rulings expanding Medical Termination of Pregnancy (MTP) rights to unmarried women.
- For Article 21 and civil liberties, maintain a repository of four to five benchmark cases—such as *Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India* establishing substantive "due process of law"—and weave them directly into arguments.
- In International Relations, enrich standard diplomatic analyses by quoting official statements from the External Affairs Minister or research papers from established think tanks like the Observer Research Foundation (ORF).

In **General Studies Paper 3 (GS3)**, precision is driven by hard empirical data. Ground economic arguments in official figures extracted from the Economic Survey, Union Budget, and Reserve Bank of India (RBI) reports. For environmental and agricultural questions, cite specific government schemes, disaster management guidelines, and international climate agreements (COPs).

In **General Studies Paper 4 (GS4 - Ethics)**, candidates frequently write abstract philosophical platitudes that fail to impress evaluators. High ethics marks demand fresh, authentic, real-world case studies drawn from daily news observation:
- Rather than reciting generic definitions of courage, cite the real-world instance of a railway police constable diving onto active tracks to rescue a falling passenger.
- For empathy and public compassion, cite citizens lining up overnight to donate blood following the Balasore train tragedy.
- For civic virtue and duty, cite grassroots civil society women in Rajasthan organizing voluntary voter awareness seminars for rural female citizens.
These contemporary exemplars prove to the evaluator that you possess authentic moral observation rather than regurgitated coaching theory.

For my **Optional Subject (Sociology)**, I maintained exceptional consistency across all three attempts, scoring approximately **280** in 2020, **278** in 2022, and **283** in 2023. My baseline notes originated from an online foundational course with IAS Gurukul, which I revised relentlessly. 

Following my first Prelims clearance, I executed a disciplined deconstruction of **at least 10 topper copies**, studying answer scripts from Neha Bhosle (AIR 15), Srishti Jayant Deshmukh (AIR 5), and Dhatri Reddy. From Srishti Deshmukh’s copies, I extracted elegant sociological mind maps and visual frameworks. From Neha Bhosle’s copies, I integrated high-yield conceptual terminology and contemporary sociological dynamics (e.g., feminization of agriculture, Sanskritization, Dalitization). 

In Sociology, the **80/20 Rule** governs scoring: mastering the core 20% of foundational sociological thinkers and paradigms yields 80% of your total marks (~280). Attempting to chase every obscure secondary course or fringe reading yields sharply diminishing returns.

In the **Essay Paper**, I practiced writing 10 full essays under timed conditions, submitting them for rigorous external evaluation. In recent years, UPSC essays have shifted heavily toward philosophical and abstract prompts. The key to mastering abstract essays is multi-level dimensional deconstruction: examining the core prompt across individual, familial, societal, national, international, and spiritual/ethical dimensions.

Finally, regarding the **Personality Test (Interview)**:
Aspirants are flooded with conflicting advice, yet the ultimate guide remains the official UPSC syllabus description. UPSC explicitly states what it evaluates: mental caliber, intellectual qualities, social traits, mental alertness, balance of judgment, clear and logical exposition, variety and depth of interest, and intellectual and moral integrity. 

My preparation centered on two pillars:
1. **Intensive Current Affairs Consolidation:** Creating comprehensive revision briefs covering the 10 to 15 days immediately preceding the interview date across national policy, geopolitical conflicts, and technological developments.
2. **Deep DAF (Detailed Application Form) Mastery:** Preparing exhaustive question trees for my prior service backgrounds (ICLS and IRS), my dual-state identity (born in Telangana with maternal roots in Karnataka), and my authentic personal interests (book reading and Carnatic classical music, where the interview board even invited me to sing a composition).

I leave you with one final, indispensable habit that I recommend to every serious aspirant: **The Syllabus Card Protocol**. Throughout the 100-day sprint between Prelims and Mains, I kept physical index cards printed with the official syllabus keywords sitting permanently on my study desk. I reviewed these syllabus cards every single day. 

You must memorize the official UPSC syllabus word for word. Your baseline benchmark must be: **Can you write 100 substantive, structured, high-value words on every single keyword listed in the official syllabus?** Once you master that 100-word baseline for every syllabus entry, value addition becomes effortless, and cracking this examination transforms from an unpredictable gamble into a disciplined, reproducible certainty.
